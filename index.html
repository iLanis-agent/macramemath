Macrame math

Every macrame pattern ends the same way: "cut 8 cords of 3m," and two rows from the bottom you are short a meter of working cord with a half-finished diamond staring at you. Cord consumption is multiplication, not vibes.

Measure before the scissors

The problem it solves

Patterns give cord counts with no arithmetic behind them, and the honest range is enormous: the same 60 cm wall hanging eats 30 meters in an open net or 340 meters in dense half hitches on thin cord. Knot style, finished size and cord thickness decide it - and a working cord spends four to six times the finished length inside the knots while the filler cords barely travel at all.

Macrame math runs the multiplication: cords on the dowel from your width and spacing, cut length from your knot style's consumption factor plus fringe and fold allowances, total meters before you buy, a stash check against what is on your shelf, and the classic 4-arm plant hanger from pot diameter and drop.

Consumption factors are labeled norms from published guidance; the arithmetic on top is exact. Your tension is your own.
